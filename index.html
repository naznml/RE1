The Importance of Friendship

Достықтың маңызы Важность дружбы

Бұл reading мәтіні достықтың адам өміріндегі рөлі, эмоционалдық қолдау, сенім, коммуникация және шынайы қарым-қатынас туралы. Мәтіннен нақты ақпаратты табу, негізгі ойды анықтау және себеп-салдар байланысын түсіну қажет.

Этот reading text о роли дружбы в жизни человека, эмоциональной поддержке, доверии, коммуникации и настоящих отношениях. Нужно находить specific information, понимать main idea и cause-effect relationships.

Мәтін Текст

B1 IELTS Reading Practice – Level B1

Topic: The Importance of Friendship

Friendship is one of the most valuable relationships in a person's life. A true friend is someone who supports you during difficult times and celebrates with you during happy moments. Unlike family, friends are people we choose. This makes friendship special, because it is based on trust, respect, and shared experiences.

Many studies show that people with strong friendships tend to be happier and healthier. Good friends can reduce stress, improve self-confidence, and even help you live longer. When you feel sad or lonely, having someone to talk to can make a big difference. In fact, emotional support from friends is as important as physical health.

However, building and maintaining strong friendships is not always easy. Friends may have different opinions, interests, or life goals. Misunderstandings and arguments can happen. That’s why communication is key. Listening, being honest, and showing empathy can help strengthen any relationship.

Today, technology also plays a big role in friendship. Social media and messaging apps allow people to stay in touch, even when they live far apart. While online communication is helpful, it cannot fully replace face-to-face time. Spending time together, sharing activities, or simply having a real conversation are all important parts of friendship.

In short, friendship adds joy and meaning to life. It is not about having many friends, but having a few real ones who truly care.

Living on Your Own

Жеке өмір сүру тәжірибесі Опыт самостоятельной жизни

Бұл reading мәтіні өз алдына бөлек тұру, жеке кеңістік, отбасы құндылықтары, қаржылық жауапкершілік және күнделікті тұрмыс міндеттері туралы. Мәтінде автордың Гонконгтағы жеке өмір сүру тәжірибесі сипатталады.

Мәтін Текст

B1 Reading Practice · Personal Space and Independent Living

Topic: Living on Your Own

I want to talk about my experience of moving out and living on my own. Moving out to live independently is not very common in Hong Kong. Many people still choose to live with their parents even when they have a decent job and are already in their thirties. Many of them stay with their parents until they get married.

There are several reasons for this. First, rent in Hong Kong is expensive. Many people would rather save money to buy an apartment than rent one. Second, according to traditional Chinese family values, parents are the people who brought you up. When you become an adult, you should also take good care of your parents and spend more time with them. Of course, some people are still dependent on their parents for food, clothing, household chores and other daily needs.

When I tell people about my idea of moving out, many people ask, “What do your parents say?” They assume that parents would not like their children to leave home. I do not know exactly how my parents feel about it, but I can say that they support and respect my decision. They think it is good for me to try living on my own because then I will understand whether it is best to stay with my family or not.

The most important reason for independent living is to save travelling time to work. It used to take me one hour and fifteen minutes to travel to work from my previous home. From my new apartment, it takes only thirty minutes, so I save forty-five minutes each way. I do not have to get up so early, and I also save two-thirds of my travelling cost.

In addition, I gain personal space and freedom by living independently. I make my own decisions all the time. I do not have to say whether I will go home for dinner, and I can invite my friends to come over and stay late. This freedom is one of the best parts of living on my own.

However, there are some trade-offs. If you want to rent an apartment, there is a lot of preparation to do. You have to visit many apartments to find the ideal one. You also have to negotiate with the landlord about the price, furniture, maintenance and equipment. In my case, after we moved in, we discovered that there was a leak in the kitchen sink only after the kitchen cupboard became soaked with water.

Money is the main issue when you live on your own. You have to be responsible for all expenses, such as rent, electricity, gas, water, telephone and internet bills. Therefore, you have to be well prepared and save money for your bills. Although my travelling time is much shorter, the saved time is spent on other things, such as cooking and household chores.

I think preparing and cooking food takes a significant amount of time, so I always try to make simple meals. I also have to tidy up my apartment regularly and wash my clothes. Therefore, living on my own does not really save as much time as I first expected.

Now everything has settled down. I am getting used to my new life, and I am enjoying it. I feel that moving out makes it easier for me to strike a balance between my work, social life, study and family. It may be troublesome, but it can be worth it.

Мәтін Текст

B1+ Academic Reading Practice · Family and Society

Topic: The Family in Society

Section A
The family has often been regarded as the cornerstone of society. In premodern and modern societies alike it has been seen as the most basic unit of social organization and one which carries out vital tasks, such as socializing children.

Section B
Until the 1960s few sociologists questioned the importance or the benefits of family life. Most sociologists assumed that family life was evolving as modernity progressed, and that the changes involved made the family better suited to meeting the needs of society and of family members. A particular type of family, the nuclear family, based around a two-generation household of parents and their children, was seen as well adapted to the demands of modern societies.

Section C
From the 1960s, an increasing number of critical thinkers began to question the assumption that the family was necessarily a beneficial institution. Feminists, Marxists and critical psychologists began to highlight what they saw as some of the negative effects and the “dark side” of family life. In the following decades the family was not just under attack from academic writers. Social changes also seemed to be undermining traditional families. Rising divorce rates, cohabitation before marriage, increasing numbers of single-parent families and single-person households, and other trends all suggested that individuals were basing their lives less and less around conventional families.

Мәтін Текст

B1+ Academic Reading Practice · Marriage and Relationships

Topic: Marriage and Communication

Marriage is a much-researched topic, and the way married couples communicate in particular has been the subject of many studies. These days, research into marriage often involves hours of recordings, followed by a thorough analysis of data with the help of modern software applications.

One such study analyzed five years' worth of data obtained from 750 participating couples. At the start of the study, participants who felt they were in a harmonious relationship reported having happy marriages. In other words, low levels of conflict corresponded to a perceived higher degree of happiness. At the end of the five-year period, however, many of these couples had separated or had started divorce proceedings. The outcome of this study suggests that keeping the peace rather than talking about problems and working through them can have harmful effects on a relationship.

In a more recent, larger-scale study, people were observed over a fifteen-year period. The researchers recorded the timings of marriages, divorces and remarriages and discovered patterns that helped them estimate how likely divorce was. If participants admitted the possibility of divorce to themselves during the first year of the study, the probability of it actually happening was ten times greater than for those couples who had not thought about it at all. Clearly, once the idea of divorce is in somebody's mind, they are more likely to act on it.

Yet another piece of research confirms that the way men and women feel at the beginning of their marriage makes a difference to its eventual outcome. Those who feel disappointed, perhaps because marriage itself is different from their expectations, or because their lifestyle is not what they had envisaged, are more likely to divorce.

Мәтін Текст

B1 Reading Practice · Business and Work

Topic: Self-Service Shopping

When the first Piggly Wiggly store opened in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1916, it was the start of a shopping revolution. It was the first self-service grocery store. That meant that customers came to the store, took the products from the shelves, and placed them in a metal basket. They then went to a cash desk to pay for the products. Shopping like that may seem familiar, because that is how many of us shop every day. However, in those days it was a completely new experience. To understand how new it was, you need to know how people had shopped before then.

Usually, stores had a counter, with the customer on one side and the storekeeper on the other. All the products were on shelves behind the storekeeper. The customer had to ask the storekeeper, or a clerk, for each item individually, and the storekeeper then placed the item on the counter or into the customer’s bag. For items such as flour and coffee, the storekeeper or clerk had to weigh each order individually. They then had to wrap each order.

It was a very slow process, and unhappy customers spent a long time waiting in line because the storekeeper could only serve one person at a time. It was also hard to make buying decisions because customers could not usually see any price information.

That all began to change when Clarence Saunders opened the first Piggly Wiggly store. He introduced a number of things that are still with us today. First of all, there was a clear entrance and exit so that customers all took the same path through the store. The products were placed on shelves where customers could choose what they wanted. Each product had a price tag on it so that customers could clearly see how much it was.

Metal baskets were provided for each customer to carry the products through the store. For the customer, it meant less waiting time and the ability to compare prices. For the storekeeper, it meant that many more customers could be served at the same time. The idea was a success, and soon many more Piggly Wiggly stores opened around the country.

Topic: Service Workers Are the Happiest Staff

New research shows that cleaners are some of the happiest workers in Britain, along with child-carers, medical secretaries, hairdressers and petrol pump attendants. The study of 35,000 employees found that the highest levels of job satisfaction were among those providing personal service, even if the pay is poor.

There are highly paid professional and managerial staff who enjoy excellent pay and conditions, but many of these appear to hate their jobs. Civil servants lie almost at the bottom of the league table, with solicitors and various engineers and scientists not far behind.

Michael Rose of Bath University, who led the study, said a key element of job satisfaction was the potential to work part-time and unsupervised. A cleaner's job may not appeal to all, but it has hidden perks. “You can trim your hours and you don't have a supervisor breathing down your neck all the time,” he said.

Petrol pump attendants, too, had a choice of working part-time shifts with relatively little direct supervision. One explanation offered for the high job-satisfaction rate of medical secretaries — at the top of the league with 75% of them happy in their job — is that they are “doing something useful”.

Seeing that you are playing a key role in the delivery of healthcare is very satisfying, despite the low pay scale of £8,000–£15,000. The study divides overall satisfaction into material satisfaction, such as money, promotion and security, and quality satisfaction, which involves the job itself, relations with the boss and working hours.

Мәтін Текст

B1+ Reading Practice · Travel and Cultural Experience

Topic: Travelling Through India

Indian rail travel is unavoidably dirty, and a balance must be struck between having the window shutters open to see the countryside and closing them against the heat and dust. The air-conditioned carriages are generally comfortable, and the train catering manager takes orders, which he passes by phone to the next stop but two, where the food comes aboard in metal dishes.

An excellent vegetarian meal of two curries, rice, pickle, nan bread, poppadoms and lassi was more than enough for my wife and me, and did not break the bank at 16 rupees. Rice plantations in the heat of the day gave way to cotton and maize fields in the evening, where farm workers stood on high stools cleaning the rice.

Our shower, breakfast and bed in the West End Hotel at Bangalore were never more welcome. The silks, silk shirts and ties in Mahatma Gandhi Road were irresistible when offered at half, or sometimes even a third, of European prices, and comfortable, well-made leather sandals were of equally good value.

The drive to Mysore took us via a silk farm, and families of monkeys began to appear at the roadside as the country became wilder. The Sultan Tipu’s summer house outside Mysore is a spacious monument to nineteenth-century good taste, but it sinks to one-star status in comparison with the Maharajah's main palace in the town.

Overwhelming in size and splendour, endless wealth has been spent on the palace over the years for the best that money could buy anywhere on earth. Its 100,000 lightbulb outline illumination is equally impressive after dark, and was extended for an extra half-hour, presumably at the taxpayer’s expense, in honour of the Indian Finance Minister’s visit the night we were there.

Our driver broke the onward journey south at an ancient Hindu temple where we were warmly welcomed to join the service. After crossing the border from Karnataka into Kerala in the Mudumalai animal reserve, the road started its long climb into the hills. Through eucalyptus woods and tea plantations, the air became cooler, the roadside greener and the lakes more frequent.

We reached the Fernhill Palace Hotel at Udagamandalam, otherwise known as Ooty, in the early afternoon, left our driver and his car and, as if in a time machine, stepped back sixty years. Empty apart from ourselves and another couple, this former Maharajah's residence was a ghost house of faded colonial gentility.

The ballroom with its padlocked grand piano, the drawing room, the dining room, the billiard room, the bar and the Maharajah's suite were all designed on the grand scale of half a century ago. Photographs along the corridors show the self-confidence of Ooty’s expatriate society between the wars; today, they present a dusty canvas of distant memories.

Whether you are travelling to Thailand’s islands or mountains, you will probably spend at least one night in Bangkok. The city can be noisy and polluted, but it is also exciting and full of things to see and do.

Khao San Road is a famous traveller area, but it is noisy and not very Thai. For a more authentic experience, Phra Kanong is a good alternative. Food is easy to find. Thai street food is famous, filling and inexpensive. In Chinatown, especially on Yaowarat Street, visitors can choose from many Chinese and Thai restaurants and food stands. Popular sights include Wat Pho, the Grand Palace, Chatuchak market and the snake farm.

Keeping Lost Weight Off

Салмақты ұзақ уақыт сақтау Как сохранить сниженный вес

Мәтін Текст

B1+ Reading Practice · Healthy Lifestyle

Topic: Keeping Lost Weight Off: The Key to Long-Term Weight Management

One of the biggest challenges faced by people who have lost weight is keeping it off. Successful long-term weight management requires an understanding of how to balance food intake and physical activity, but it is also about more than that. The key is finding a sustainable lifestyle that you enjoy.

To begin with, it is crucial to understand that maintaining your weight does not mean reverting to your old eating habits once you have reached your weight loss goal. The diet that helped you lose weight should be the foundation of your eating habits moving forward. Of course, you can have some occasional indulgences, but these should be the exception rather than the rule.

Physical activity is another crucial component of long-term weight management. Regular exercise helps burn calories, but it also improves your physical health in many other ways, making you feel better overall and boosting your motivation to maintain a healthy lifestyle.

One often overlooked aspect of keeping lost weight off is the role of social support. Having supportive people around you — family, friends, or support groups — can make a huge difference. They can provide encouragement during tough times and celebrate with you when you achieve your milestones.

Maintaining your weight also requires regular monitoring. This does not necessarily mean weighing yourself every day. Becoming obsessed with your weight is not a healthy habit to develop, but keeping an eye on your weight trends can help identify small weight gains early, so they can be addressed before they turn into larger problems.

Another important factor in weight maintenance is mental health. Stress and depression can often lead to overeating and reduced physical activity. Learning how to manage stress and seeking help for depression can be crucial components of keeping off the weight you have lost.

In conclusion, successful long-term weight management is about balance. It requires a combination of healthy eating, regular physical activity, social support, regular monitoring, and good mental health. It is a lifestyle change, but it is one that comes with many rewards.

Мәтін Текст

B1+ Reading Practice · Healthy Lifestyle and Wellbeing

Topic: Mental and Physical Wellbeing

No one is immune to developing chronic illnesses such as heart disease, cancer or arthritis as they get older. However, research shows that social activities, such as joining clubs, interest groups or volunteering, are linked to better mental and physical health and a longer lifespan.

Recent research found that the more people participated in social activities, the lower their risk was of developing or accumulating chronic conditions. Researchers looked at people aged fifty and older from twelve European countries over a five-year period. They studied how volunteering, education, joining a club, or being involved in religious or political groups influenced the likelihood of developing major chronic illnesses.

The study found that weekly participation in social activities reduced the risk of developing a chronic condition by eight percent compared to no participation. It also reduced the risk of developing two or more chronic conditions by twenty-two percent. Even for busy people, the findings suggest that just a few hours spent on a social activity every week can protect health.

Social activities are important not only because they help people stay physically active, but also because doing activities with other people benefits mental wellbeing. Better mental wellbeing can, in turn, further protect physical health.

Taking time to be inspired by new things is also good for health. Studies show that people who read books live longer, and bilingual people have better cognitive health. Pursuing new knowledge or learning new skills is known to promote wellbeing and memory function.

Activities such as attending an art or music class are associated with enhanced brain health because they improve communication between different brain regions. They can also improve psychological resilience, which means people become better able to cope with and persevere through stressful or challenging situations.

People who have made a habit of learning throughout their lifetime generally have better physical and mental health. They may have a reduced risk of heart disease and obesity, healthier habits such as good nutrition, exercise and not smoking, better wellbeing and cognition, and a stronger sense of purpose in life.
